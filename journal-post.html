The Language of Thread: How We Choose Every Colour

July 20, 2026  ·  By Ritik Parihar  ·  6 min read

Our thread library holds forty-eight colours, and every one of them earned its place the same way — by surviving a hoop. A shade doesn't join the shelf because it looks good in a skein; it joins because it still looks right after six hours under studio light, stitched into linen, next to the three colours it will actually be paired with.

Antique gold was one of the first to pass that test, and it has quietly appeared in some form in nearly every collection since. It reads warm against ivory, doesn't fight with deeper tones like maroon or midnight, and photographs consistently whether it's morning or evening light in the studio.

"A colour has to work on cloth, not just on paper. That's the whole test."

Building a palette, not a rainbow

When we design a new collection, we rarely start with colour. We start with the motif, work it in a neutral thread first, and only then ask which two or three colours will carry it. Restraint matters more than range — most of our pieces use no more than four thread colours total.

It's a slower way to build a colour library than simply buying what's trending, but it means every shade in our studio has a job. Nothing sits on the shelf unused, and nothing goes into a hoop just because it's new.

What's next

We're currently testing two new shades for an upcoming festive collection — a deeper terracotta and a muted plum. Both are already a few weeks into the hoop test. We'll let you know which one survives.
